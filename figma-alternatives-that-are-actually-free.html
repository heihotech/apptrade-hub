Figma alternatives that are actually free

Figma's free plan used to be genuinely generous. Then came the 2022 changes, and suddenly teams found themselves hitting draft limits, losing editor seats, and staring at a $15/month per-editor bill. If you're a solo designer, a student, or a small startup watching the budget, that adds up fast. The good news: there are real alternatives worth your time — not just "free trials" dressed up as free tools. Here's an honest look at what's actually out there.

What "Actually Free" Means Here

For this list, a tool qualifies if it has a free tier that's usable long-term — not a 30-day trial, not a "free" plan that locks 90% of features behind a paywall. Some tools here are fully open source. Others have commercial free tiers with real limits, but limits you can realistically work within.

Penpot — The Open Source Contender

Penpot is the most direct Figma alternative on this list. It's open source, browser-based, and completely free. You can use the hosted version at penpot.app or self-host it on your own server if you want full control over your data.

The interface will feel familiar if you've used Figma — you get frames, components, constraints, and a prototype mode. The big differentiator is that Penpot uses SVG as its native format, which makes exporting and integrating with development workflows cleaner in some cases.

Honest tradeoff: The component system isn't as mature as Figma's, and the plugin ecosystem is still growing. For complex design systems, you'll notice the gaps. But for UI mockups, wireframes, and collaborating with a small team? It holds up well.

Best for: Teams that care about open source, privacy-conscious organizations, and developers who want to self-host their design tooling.

Lunacy — Offline-First and Free

Lunacy by Icons8 is a desktop app for Windows, Mac, and Linux that's entirely free — no subscription, no feature gating. It started as a Sketch file viewer but has grown into a full design tool with its own asset library baked in.

What makes Lunacy stand out is its offline capability. You don't need an internet connection to design, which matters if you work on a laptop with inconsistent connectivity. It also ships with free built-in icons, photos, and illustrations from Icons8, which is a practical time-saver for quick mockups.

Honest tradeoff: Real-time collaboration isn't as smooth as Figma's, and the community around it is smaller, meaning fewer tutorials and shared resources. It's also not browser-based, so there's a download required.

Best for: Solo designers who prefer desktop apps, Windows users, and anyone who needs to work offline regularly.

Framer — Free for Personal Projects

Framer sits in an interesting middle ground between design and website building. Its free plan lets you publish one site and work on projects without paying, which is genuinely useful for portfolio work or landing page mockups.

The interaction and animation capabilities are strong — arguably better than Figma's prototype mode for high-fidelity interactive demos. If you're designing something that needs to feel alive, Framer is worth a look.

Honest tradeoff: Framer leans toward web publishing, so it's less useful for pure UI design like mobile app screens. The free tier also puts a Framer badge on published sites, which is fine for personal work but awkward for client projects.

Best for: Designers building portfolio sites or interactive web prototypes.

Vectr — Simple and No-Signup-Required Editing

Vectr is lightweight, browser-based, and free without any account required for basic use. It's not trying to replace Figma for complex product design — it's a simple vector editor that works well for icons, logos, and basic illustrations.

Best for: Beginners, non-designers who need to make quick graphics, and anyone who wants zero setup friction.

A Quick Comparison

  • Penpot — Best overall Figma alternative; open source, collaborative, browser-based
  • Lunacy — Best for offline work and solo designers on desktop
  • Framer — Best for interactive prototypes and web-focused design
  • Vectr — Best for simple vector work with zero setup

Should You Actually Switch?

If your team is already deep in Figma — with shared libraries, plugins, and developer handoff workflows — switching has a real cost. Don't underestimate it. For many established teams, staying on Figma's paid plan is probably still the right call.

But if you're starting fresh, working solo, or just can't justify the cost right now, Penpot in particular has reached a point where it's a legitimate daily driver. It's not feature-identical to Figma, but it's closer than most people expect — and the gap is shrinking with each release.

The honest verdict: the best free Figma alternative depends entirely on your workflow. Try Penpot first. If that doesn't fit, Lunacy is your next stop. Neither will feel exactly like Figma, but both will get the job done without the monthly bill.
